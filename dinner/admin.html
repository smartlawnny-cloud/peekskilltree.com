01

The problem

"What's for dinner" is asked ~1,800 times per household per decade. The answers got worse, not better.

Delivery apps don't answer the question

DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub show 200 restaurants ranked by ad spend. The user has to filter, decide, re-decide, abandon the cart twice, and finally pay $34 for tacos that arrive cold. Decision fatigue compounds the cost of food, time, and dignity.

Meal kits solve the wrong week

HelloFresh, Blue Apron, Sunbasket plan next week's dinners — but most households want tonight's. Subscription lock-in, national warehouses, plastic-wrapped portions shipped 1,200 miles.

Recipe apps don't know your fridge

Allrecipes, NYT Cooking, Yummly are great if you already know what you want to make. They're useless at 6:45pm when you're looking at a half-bag of spinach, three eggs, and chicken thighs.

Local food has no consumer surface

The farm 1.4 miles away with extra eggs, the neighbor who rolled too many perogies, the meal-prep operator one town over — they exist, but there's no app for them. Farmers' markets are Saturday only.

02

The solution

A single-question app. Five screens. The default screen is tonight's answer.

Fridge-first

Camera + vision model reads what's on the shelves. Recipe engine ranks meals by ingredient overlap and time-to-spoil (prioritizes the spinach). The app's first instinct is always "what do you already have."

Hyperlocal market

Curated directory of farms, home cooks, and meal-prep operators within 4 miles. Operators set their own inventory daily. Pickup at skate-park lockers — open 24/7, code unlocks at the moment of pickup. No delivery driver, no fee, no algorithm.

Calm budget

User sets a monthly food number. App shows where it went, what's left. Same monochrome aesthetic as the rest of the app. Eating out tracked separately. No alerts, no shaming.

Memory layer

Onboarding captures 1–3 favorites. The app surfaces them when ingredient overlap aligns. "743 days since you made Mom's perogies — Maria's Kitchen has a dozen left today." Not nostalgia-bait. Just memory.

04

Revenue model

Three layered streams. The free tier funnels into all three.

1. Household subscription · $6/mo

Unlocks: fridge scans beyond 5/mo, multi-person profiles, receipt auto-import, favorites memory beyond 3. Free tier remains usable forever — no dark patterns.

  • Estimated 18% free→paid conversion
  • Strong retention (food is recurring)

2. Operator take · 12%

Each transaction through a local operator (farm, home cook, meal-prep) takes 12%. Lower than DoorDash's ~30%, higher than nothing. Operators keep their own pricing and inventory.

  • Stripe Connect for payouts (weekly)
  • Locker pickup, no delivery costs

3. Locker placement · $80/mo

Locker hosts (skate parks, cafes, community centers) get $40 of that as foot traffic / lease share. Net $40 per locker per month.

  • 1 locker serves ~80 households
  • Asset-light: lockers, not warehouses

What we won't do

No ad revenue. No selling user data. No "DashPass" tier that pushes paid search results. The product breaks if the feed sells attention.

  • No sponsored recipes
  • No paid operator placement

Boring, fast, cheap. Same instincts as the Branch Manager build.

Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS, no framework. PWA-installable on iOS + Android. Camera API for fridge scans. Service worker for offline.

08

Risks & mitigations

The honest list.

Cottage-food laws vary by state
NY requires Home Processor's License for prepared food sold. Operators must register; the app gates listing on uploaded license. Farms are exempt for raw produce.
Vision model misidentifies fridge contents
Manual edit always one tap away. We log misidentifications + retrain prompts. Worst case is "you have spinach you don't have" — recoverable.
Operator supply < household demand
Beta starts operator-first — recruit 5 before opening to households. Each operator covers ~15–20 households comfortably. Daily inventory toggle prevents oversell.
Locker theft / vandalism
Pilot lockers at staffed locations (skate park during open hours, cafes). 24/7 access via PIN, expires at pickup window end. Camera on locker housing. $80/mo includes minor replacement budget.
DoorDash / Instacart copies feature
They can't copy the network. Hyperlocal operator relationships are slow, manual, trust-based — exactly the kind of moat a $30B logistics company won't build.
Solo founder bottleneck
AI-assisted ops from day one. Operator onboarding semi-automated by Q3. Doug+AI workflow already battle-tested on Branch Manager (sister project, same stack).
