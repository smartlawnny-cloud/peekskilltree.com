It opens to one screen. One question. Tonight's answer.

Everything else hangs off that. No newsfeed. No restaurants ranked by who paid the most.

  • Scan your fridge with the camera — it reads the shelves, lists what's there, suggests three meals you can make right now.
  • If you're missing one or two things, it surfaces a neighbor or local farm 1–4 miles away that has it today.
  • The budget tracker is the same color palette as the rest of the app. Not a scary spreadsheet. Just where the money's been.
  • Favorites remembers what you used to love and stopped making. "743 days since you've had Mom's perogies."

What's inside

Five screens. Nothing else.

We didn't add a social feed, a gamified streak system, or AI-generated meal plans for next month. Just the five things you actually use.

🏠

Home

Tonight's answer, the four things you do most, and the connections you've wired up. That's the whole screen.

📷

Fridge

Hold your phone up. Vision model reads the shelves. You get a list of ingredients and three meals that use them — including one prioritized to use the spinach before it turns.

🌾

Market

Hyperlocal directory of farms, home cooks, and meal-prep operators within 4 miles. Pickup at a skate-park locker. No delivery fee, no driver tip, no algorithm.

$

Budget

You set a number. The app shows where it went — groceries, local market, eating out — and what's left. Same monochrome aesthetic. Calm, not anxious.

♡

Favorites

Add the meals you love during onboarding. The app reminds you when you haven't made them in a while and ingredients line up. Not nostalgia-bait. Just memory.

🛹

Skate-park lockers

Pickup hubs at local plazas — open 24/7, code unlocks at the moment of pickup. The hyperlocal piece that makes neighbor-to-neighbor food actually work.

Why we built it

The dinner question used to be answered by your block.

Maria across the street made too many perogies. The farm at the end of the road sold eggs on the honor system. Your neighbor's dad smoked brisket on Saturdays and gave half of it away.

Then we replaced all of that with apps that ship boxes from New Jersey and restaurants priced for a tip a stranger keeps. The app should put the block back.

I don't want to scroll seventeen ramen places at 6:45pm. I want to know what I have, what my neighbor has, and what I can afford. That's it.
— the design brief
